## 1.1 About this guide
This is a SYNTHETIC supplement for the fictional Veloxa Volt. It explains how cold weather affects range and charging. It is for demonstration only and must not be used for any real vehicle.

## 1.2 Range in cold weather
In temperatures below 0 °C the usable driving range can drop by up to 30% compared with mild weather. The heater and the battery heater use energy from the HV battery.
To reduce the loss, use the seat and steering wheel heaters instead of high cabin heat, and keep the tyres at the pressure shown on the driver's door label.

## 1.3 Preconditioning
Preconditioning warms the cabin and the HV battery before you drive. Set a departure time in the Veloxa app or on the centre screen under Climate > Departure.
Preconditioning works best while the vehicle is plugged in, because the energy then comes from the charger instead of the battery. Start it at least 30 minutes before departure when the temperature is below -10 °C.

## 1.4 Charging in sub-zero temperatures
A cold HV battery charges more slowly. Below -10 °C, DC fast charging power is limited until the battery has warmed up. AC home charging is not affected as much.
To charge faster on a road trip, set the DC fast charger as the navigation destination: the Volt then warms the battery on the way, about 20 minutes before arrival.

## 1.5 Frozen charge port
If the charge port cover is frozen, do not force it open and do not pour hot water on it. Switch on preconditioning for 15 minutes to warm the port area, then try again.
If the charging cable is locked in a frozen port, use the manual cable release in the front luggage compartment.

## 1.6 Battery heater warning light (amber) [safety]
Meaning: The HV battery heater has a fault, so the battery may not warm up in cold weather. Range and charging power may be reduced.
Required action: You can continue to drive with care. Avoid DC fast charging below -10 °C and contact Veloxa service to have the battery heater checked. Do not open or repair any HV component yourself.

## 1.7 Storing the vehicle in winter
If the Volt will not be driven for more than 2 weeks in freezing weather, leave it plugged in with the charge limit set to 70%. This keeps the battery warm enough and protects the 12 V battery.
